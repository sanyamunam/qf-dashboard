# R2 layout rationale — the three arrangements considered (§2, §8.14)

**The problem:** five themes, wildly unequal weight (Social Progress 61 indicators / 4 entities; Artificial Intelligence 2 / 1). A uniform 5-up row denies the asymmetry; strict weight-proportional sizing punishes young themes.

## Considered

**A. Hero plus four — CHOSEN.** The theme carrying the most consequential finding (Social Progress: the WISH beneficiary collapse, the largest movement in the dataset) takes a large card on the left; the other four sit in a 2×2 to its right, identical to each other. Why it wins: it gives the page the single focal point it lacked (§2.2 constraint: one dominant element), it is honest about where the CEO's attention belongs this quarter without permanently ranking themes (the hero slot is earned by the spotlight engine, not assigned to a theme), and the 1 + 4 arrangement resolves the awkward five. The AI brief sits above as a 2–3 line band, clearly supporting rather than competing.

**B. 2 + 3 rows — rejected.** Generous cards, but no hierarchy: five peers in two rows leaves the brief and the card field competing for dominance, recreating the exact balance problem R2 names. The uneven row split also reads as an accident rather than a decision (which row gets three? why?).

**C. Editorial column — rejected.** Brief as a left column with a card grid right makes the AI summary a permanent panel, which §2.2 explicitly forbids ("a brief, not a panel"), and at 1024 it compresses five cards into a column too narrow for the four visual grammars to breathe.

## The chosen structure at 1024×700

Header (logo + period, ~64px) → AI brief band (~120px, under a fifth of the screen) → hero card + 2×2 (~460px) → floating nav pill. The Organizational Excellence banner sits just below the fold — an accepted trade per §2.2; it is an enabling function, not a thematic area. Hero and small cards each keep a fixed internal grid (label row / chip / figure / sentence / visual / CTA) so baselines align across peers within a tier.
